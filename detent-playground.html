Constraints

Three ways to limit where something can go. Each is one option on the same call.

Locked to one axis

Moves horizontally only. Vertical travel is ignored rather than resisted.

x only

Snapped to a grid

Rounds to the nearest 20px, counted from the field's edge so it lands on the lines you can see.

20px

Caged in its parent

Stops at every edge, and stays stopped however far the pointer goes.

bounded

Grips and dead zones

This card only moves by its grip, and the button inside it still takes clicks. A drag that ends over a button never fires that button's click.

Reorder a list

Items slide out of the way as you pass them. Nothing is animated through layout — the browser measures once and the rest is a transform.

    A row of chips

    The same call. The direction is worked out from where the items actually sit, so nothing needs configuring.

      A wrapping grid

      Once items wrap onto more than one line, the nearest tile decides the drop rather than a single midpoint. Resize your window and it keeps up.

      Across three lists

      Lists that share a group name pass items to each other. Drop into an empty column and it takes the card.

      A list that scrolls

      Drag a card towards the top or bottom edge and the list scrolls to meet you, faster the closer you get. On touch this list still swipes normally — press and hold to lift instead.

        Without a mouse

        Tab to an item, then press space to lift it, arrow keys to move it and space again to drop. Escape puts it back. Each step is announced to screen readers.

          Tab to focus · Space lift or drop · ↑ ↓ move · Esc cancel

          All of it at once

          Four panels on a canvas. Each one moves by its title bar and resizes from its bottom-right corner, both caged inside the canvas. Two of them hold sortable lists that pass cards between each other while the panels themselves are being moved.
